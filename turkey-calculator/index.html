Frequently asked questions

Are these times a guarantee that the turkey is done?

No. USDA says the timetables are approximate. Always use a food thermometer. The turkey is safe only when it reaches 165°F in the innermost part of the thigh and wing and the thickest part of the breast.

When should I move a frozen turkey to the fridge?

This page uses the longer end of the USDA refrigerator chart for the bird’s weight (for example, 3 days for 4–12 lb). Put it in the refrigerator on that date, still in its wrapper, on a tray. If it thaws early, USDA says a thawed turkey can remain in the refrigerator 1 to 2 days.

Should I stuff the turkey?

USDA does not recommend stuffing a turkey for optimum safety. Cook stuffing in a casserole. If you stuff the bird, mix wet and dry ingredients just before filling the cavities loosely, cook immediately, and check that the center of the stuffing reaches 165°F.

What if my turkey is bigger than 24 pounds?

The USDA thaw and roast charts on this page go up to 24 pounds. We do not invent times beyond the chart. You can still use “about 24 hours for every 4 to 5 pounds” and “about 30 minutes per pound” for thawing. For roasting, call the USDA Meat and Poultry Hotline at 1-888-674-6854, or follow the package.
