How the math works

StepRule
1. Sale % offTaken off the original price. Percents over 100 are treated as 100.
2. Extra % couponSequential (stacked): the coupon applies to the already-reduced price. $100 with 25% off then 10% off is $67.50.
Added: the two percents are added, then applied once to the original, capped at 100%. The same numbers become $65.
3. Dollar offSubtracted after the percentages. The price will not go below $0.
4. Sales taxApplied to the discounted price, then rounded to the nearest cent. Total savings and effective discount are pre-tax, versus the original price. Some jurisdictions tax the pre-discount price; this page does not try to model that.

Each percentage discount is rounded to the nearest cent before the next step (half-up). A $19.99 item at 30% off is $13.99. A store’s register may round differently.

Frequently asked questions

Which stacking method should I pick?

Use the one that matches the coupon’s fine print, if you have it. “An extra 10% off” often means sequential (10% of the already-reduced price). Some promotions add the percentages. This page shows both so you can see the difference. It cannot tell you how a particular store will ring up the sale.

Are the default numbers a real Black Friday deal?

No. They are a worked example ($100, 25% off, 10% coupon) so you can see sequential vs added on the same inputs. Replace them with the prices and discounts in front of you.

Is the sales tax figure official?

No. This is not tax advice and not a tax table. If you enter a rate, it is applied to the discounted price. Your local rules may differ, including whether tax is charged on the pre-discount price.

When is Black Friday 2026?

Friday, November 27, 2026 — the day after U.S. Thanksgiving (Thursday, November 26).
